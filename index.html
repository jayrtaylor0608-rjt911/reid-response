Most seizures end on their own. Your calm actions can make a tremendous difference.

This page will walk you through exactly what to do.

If Reid is actively having a seizure
Follow these steps in order.
1

Stay calm

Take a deep breath. Stay with Reid. Remain calm — your presence matters.

2

Protect Reid from injury

Move furniture or dangerous objects out of the way. Protect his head. If possible, gently roll him onto his side once the seizure ends.

✕ Do not hold him down
✕ Do not put anything in his mouth
✕ Do not try to stop the seizure
3

Start timing

Use your phone's clock or timer. Knowing exactly how long the seizure lasts is critical for the next steps.

4

Use Reid's VNS magnet

The VNS magnet is attached to Reid's Emergency Response Kit (lanyard or backpack), next to his emergency medication — the same kit this QR code is attached to.

The VNS device is implanted just below Reid's left collarbone. It works through clothing — there's no need to expose the device.

Activate: Place the magnet over the implanted device and move it in a continuous figure-8 (∞) motion while slowly counting to five. Don't try to straighten, hold down, or restrain Reid — work around his natural movements while keeping him safe.

Remove: The stimulation begins once the magnet is moved away from the device.

Repeat if needed: If the seizure continues after 60 seconds, repeat the figure-8 swipe. If it continues for another 60 seconds, repeat a third time.

Maximum: Three VNS magnet activations per seizure event, or follow Reid's neurologist's specific instructions.

5

Emergency medication

Reid carries emergency seizure medication (Nayzilam).

If the seizure lasts longer than 3 minutes despite VNS magnet use, follow Reid's Seizure Action Plan and administer his prescribed rescue medication.

Nayzilam 10mg Nasal Spray — 2 units at 5mg per unit.

Locate the Nayzilam. If Reid is still seizing after 3 minutes, administer it nasally, then call 911.

Tell 911 the medication has been administered, and whether he is still actively seizing or has stopped.

6

Call 911 if:

  • The seizure lasts longer than 3 minutes — administer Nayzilam and call
  • Another seizure begins before he fully recovers
  • He is injured
  • He has difficulty breathing after the seizure
  • You are uncomfortable or unsure
Call 911 now

You will never upset us by calling 911. We would much rather have help on the way than have someone hesitate over worrying about overreacting.

After the seizure
This is called the recovery period. Reid may be:
Sleepy
Confused
Very tired
Sore
Have a headache
Not remember what happened

Stay with him. Speak calmly. Offer reassurance. Do not rush him.
